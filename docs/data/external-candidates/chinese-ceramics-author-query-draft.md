**Subject:** Clarification on assemblage records and classifications in your Chinese trade ceramics dataset

Dear Dr. Zhang,

We are preparing undergraduate teaching materials in Chinese for an introductory textbook on AI in archaeology. We are considering using your openly published dataset accompanying *Chinese ceramics as global commodities: a thousand years of production and trade of Chinese ceramics in the Western Indian Ocean* (DOI: 10.6084/m9.figshare.27842990), with full attribution and a clear account of its limitations.

We have checked the supplementary workbook and would appreciate clarification of three points before preparing teaching materials:

1. In `Part 3 – Chinese Ceramic Finds`, seven Site ID–Class ID combinations occur under different Assemblage IDs (six at Kinolhas and one at Suakin). Do these reflect distinct source publications, field seasons, recording units or another distinction? Should they remain separate in quantitative summaries, and is there a risk of double-counting if they are aggregated?
2. The article's introduction describes 12 product types and 869 assemblages, while the abstract/other sections and the supplementary workbook indicate 15 product types and 896 assemblage records. Is there a version history, revised classification or correction that explains these differences?
3. Would you recommend any additional precautions when preparing a small teaching derivative of the data, particularly with respect to provenance, category definitions, chronological weighting and reuse of site-level geographic descriptions?

We intend to distinguish assemblage records from individual sherd counts, retain original identifiers and document every transformation. We would be grateful for a brief clarification or a pointer to any existing methodological documentation.

With thanks for making the underlying research available.
